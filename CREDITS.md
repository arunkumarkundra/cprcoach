# Credits and licences

Attribution appears here and in `README.md`, never on an emergency screen — nothing
displays during a resuscitation that does not help the rescuer act. This satisfies
CC BY, which requires credit "in any reasonable manner" but does not require it on
the image itself.

### `Infant_Mouth_to_Mouth-and-Nose_Breathing.png` — infant ventilation

- **Source:** generated by an LLM at the maintainer's request
- **Licence:** no third-party rights asserted

**On AI-generated images.** In most jurisdictions, including the US, a purely
machine-generated image attracts no copyright, so there is no third party to credit and
no licence to comply with. Two residual risks are worth naming rather than ignoring:

1. A generator can occasionally reproduce recognisable elements of a training image. If
   either picture ever turns out to closely resemble a specific published illustration,
   replace it.
2. **Generated medical illustrations can be confidently wrong.** They must be checked
   against the protocol by someone clinically qualified — this matters far more than the
   licensing question.

Both current images were checked against the spoken instruction and match it:
two fingers on the lower sternum just below the nipple line, approximately 4 cm
(1.5 inch) depth, neutral infant airway, and a combined mouth-and-nose seal.

**One image corrected the app.** The ventilation illustration specifies *"neutral or
slight head-tilt"* for an infant. The app was telling every rescuer to tilt the head
back, which in an infant closes the airway. Infants now receive their own instruction
set (`say.bi1`–`bi3`). This is exactly the review value good imagery provides.

## Illustrations that would still help

In priority order. Each is a slot already wired into `MEDIA` — add the file, add one
line, done.

| Priority | Slot | Subject | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `pads` | AED pad placement on a bare adult chest — one upper right below the collarbone, one lower left below the armpit | Pad placement is the step people most often get wrong, and the AED cannot correct them |
| 2 | `recovery` | Recovery position — on the side, head tilted back, mouth pointing down, upper knee bent forward | Currently text only, and the position is hard to picture from words |
| 3 | `tilt` | Adult head-tilt chin-lift, side view, one hand on forehead, two fingers under the bony chin | Shown on the positioning screen, before compressions start |
| 4 | `breath` | Adult mouth-to-mouth, nose pinched shut, airtight seal | Only the infant version exists; adults trained in breaths currently see nothing |
| 5 | `handsChild` | One-handed compression on a child | Presently reuses the adult animation, which shows two hands |
| 6 | `flat` | Casualty flat on their back on a hard floor, rescuer kneeling beside the chest | Reinforces "not a bed, not a sofa" |
| 7 | `shoulder` | Shaking the shoulders and shouting to check response | The first action of all, currently text only |
| 8 | `chest` | Exposing the chest, clothing cut or moved aside | Low value; the instruction is already unambiguous |

### If you generate more, keep them consistent

- Same illustration style, palette and viewpoint across the set — a mixed set reads as
  unreliable.
- Landscape or square, roughly 4:3. They render at a maximum of 26% of screen height.
- One action per image. No numbered sub-steps, no multi-panel layouts.
- **Never invent numbers.** Any depth or rate written on an image must match the app text
  exactly: adult about 5 cm, child about one third of chest depth, infant about 4 cm,
  rate 100–120 per minute.
- Under 200 KB where possible.
- Anatomically plausible hands. Generators are unreliable at hands, and a picture showing
  the wrong finger count on an infant's chest is a safety defect, not a cosmetic one.

### On the burnt-in English text

Both current images carry English captions. A Tamil-speaking rescuer sees English they
may not read, which breaks the multilingual promise and adds words that do not help them
act. This is acceptable for a beta and should be resolved before release, either by
cropping the captions or by regenerating without text.

## Illustrations no longer used

The ten hand-drawn SVG line drawings previously shipped with the app were original work,
released CC0, and have been removed. They were ambiguous, and an ambiguous diagram is
worse than none.
